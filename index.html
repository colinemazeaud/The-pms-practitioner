How I Work

It's rarely just hormones.

Information isn't the missing piece anymore. Anyone can find a list of foods that help PMS. What's missing is someone looking at your specific history and telling you what's actually going on — that's the part no article, AI chatbot, or Instagram reel can do.

PMS symptoms are usually a signal, not a stand-alone problem. Your gut, your blood sugar and your nervous system all influence how your hormones behave. A symptom in the days before your period can trace back to something happening weeks earlier, somewhere else in your body entirely.

That's why every plan starts with your whole picture — medical history, nutrition, mental health, lifestyle, environment — not just your cycle in isolation. I'm also a certified Menstrual Cycle Awareness Coach, so I look at how your whole cycle is functioning, not only the week you struggle with. The same approach applies for the women I work with on endometriosis and PMOS.

Years of disordered eating, gut issues and mood swings no one connected — including severe PMS that made parts of every month feel unlivable. I knew what it was to not understand my own body, to feel powerless, to be told it was "just part of being a woman." It wasn't until a nutritional therapist finally asked about my full history that it clicked into a picture I'd never seen before: recurrent antibiotic use as a child, low breastfeeding, a family history of autoimmunity — small pieces no one had ever put together. My gut health and my PMS weren't separate problems. They were the same root cause, showing up in different ways. Understanding that was the first time I felt in control of my own body.

My sister's experience shaped this just as much. She also suffered from severe PMS, on top of eight years of cyclical back pain dismissed as arthritis — before anyone thought to check if it was linked to her cycle. It was endometriosis. Watching her go through that, not being able to help her, made me feel powerless, and it made all of us feel powerless with her. Supporting her also taught me my first real lesson as a practitioner: more information isn't more helpful. A woman overwhelmed with a perfect plan won't follow it. Slow, sustainable, one change at a time — explained clearly enough that she understands why it's for her, specifically — is what actually creates change. Never forget there's a person behind the case.

That's why PMS is where I focus now. I know first-hand what it is to lose a week of yourself every month — and that it's rarely just "hormones." It's usually the gut, the nervous system, and everything underneath, asking for help.

Also PMS-Adjacent

Endometriosis & PMOS get the same approach.

The Rhythm Reset works the same way for endometriosis and PMOS as it does for PMS — the same consultation, the same personalised plan, starting from your full history rather than a generic protocol.

Endometriosis isn't just a reproductive condition — it's a full-body one, involving the gut and the immune system as much as the reproductive cycle. The gut-endo connection matters here: inflammation driven by gut health can directly worsen pain severity, which is often missed when the focus stays only on the reproductive system.

PMOS isn't one condition either — there are four functional subtypes, each with a different root driver (insulin resistance, inflammation, adrenal-related, or post-pill). Generic PMOS advice tends to treat it as a single thing, which is exactly why it so often doesn't work. Figuring out which subtype you're dealing with changes what actually helps.

Before You Book

What people usually ask, or worry about

The Rhythm Reset isn't about cutting things out. It's about adding in what's missing and understanding what's driving your symptoms first — at your pace, not a list of banned foods.

Hormones don't operate in isolation. Gut health, blood sugar, inflammation and the nervous system all influence hormone symptoms. This isn't instead of medical care — it's addressing a layer that's often missed entirely.

"Healthy" isn't the same as "right for your body." You can eat well by every general standard and still have a specific imbalance that generic healthy eating doesn't touch, because it was never personalised to you.

Most things you've tried were probably general protocols, not built from your history. That's the difference here — a plan built from your specific picture, not another list to try and abandon.

This isn't a single consultation. It's a supported process — initial consult, two follow-ups, and email support — built around the fact that real change takes time and adjustment.

The wrong supplements — or the right ones taken the wrong way — can do very little, or even backfire. They need to be tailored to your physiology and checked against any medication you're on. That's exactly the kind of detail a personalised plan accounts for.
